## Brand Identity

### What PREPPY LOSERS Is

PREPPY LOSERS is a premium oversized streetwear brand that exists at the intersection of **Ivy-adjacent polish** and **underground rebellion**. The name is a deliberate paradox: "preppy" signals refinement, structure, and cultural literacy; "losers" reclaims outsider status as a badge of authenticity. Together, they describe people who look put-together but refuse to belong to anyone else's system.

The brand is not about fitting in. It is about **owning the contradiction** — looking elite while operating outside the mainstream.

### Core Identity Statement

> PREPPY LOSERS is underground street culture for people who dress with intention, move with confidence, and reject the ordinary.

### Identity Pillars

| Pillar | Definition | Why It Matters |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| **Contradiction** | The brand thrives on tension — refined vs. raw, exclusive vs. accessible, minimal vs. bold. | Creates memorability and cultural depth. Generic brands pick one lane; PREPPY LOSERS lives in the friction. |
| **Exclusivity** | Limited drops, editorial presentation, insider language. Not mass-market. | Supports premium pricing and cultivates desire through scarcity, not discounts. |
| **Self-Expression** | Clothing as identity, not uniform. Every piece is a statement. | Aligns with the target audience's need to be seen as individuals, not consumers. |
| **Craft** | Heavyweight fabrics, intentional construction, quality you can feel. | Justifies premium positioning and builds long-term trust beyond hype cycles. |
| **Underground Culture** | Rooted in street, skate, music, and subcultural scenes — not corporate fashion. | Ensures the brand feels authentic to its audience and avoids "mall brand" energy. |

### Visual Identity Direction (Summary)

While full visual specifications live in Document 02, the identity implies:

- **Dark, editorial environments** — campaign-first, not catalog-first
- **Oversized silhouettes** as a recurring visual motif
- **High-contrast typography** — confident, not playful
- **Minimal UI chrome** — the product and photography lead, not the interface

**Why:** The identity must feel like a fashion campaign you stumbled into, not a Shopify theme with a logo swapped in.

## Brand Story

### Origin Narrative

Every great streetwear brand has a mythology. PREPPY LOSERS was born from a simple observation: the most interesting people in any room are the ones who **don't follow the dress code** — but still look like they wrote it.

They wore blazers with skate shoes. They carried vintage bags with new sneakers. They looked "preppy" to outsiders and "lost" to institutions. They were never quite in — and never wanted to be.

PREPPY LOSERS is for that person. The one who was told they didn't belong, and decided that was the point.

### The Narrative Arc

1. **Rejection** — Mainstream fashion feels hollow, performative, and interchangeable.
2. **Discovery** — Underground culture offers authenticity, community, and self-definition.
3. **Expression** — Clothing becomes armor, signal, and art — not just coverage.
4. **Belonging** — PREPPY LOSERS creates a tribe of bold outsiders who recognize each other without needing a logo scream.

### Story Usage Guidelines

| Context | How to Use the Story |
|---------|---------------------|
| About page | Full narrative arc — emotional, cinematic, first-person plural ("we") |
| Product pages | Micro-stories tied to each drop or collection — never generic filler |
| Social / campaigns | Fragment the story into visual moments — a glance, a texture, a silhouette |
| Email / CRM | Insider tone — "you already know" energy, not explainer copy |
| Homepage | Implied, not told — story lives in imagery and atmosphere, not paragraphs |

**Why:** Over-explaining kills mystique. The story should feel discovered, not delivered. Underground brands earn loyalty through atmosphere and implication.

## Brand Principles

Brand Principles are the operational rules that translate values into daily decisions. Unlike values (what we believe), principles (how we act) should guide every future design, product, copy, and engineering choice.

### 1. Campaign Before Catalog

Every page — especially homepage, collection, and drop pages — must feel like entering a fashion campaign, not scrolling a product grid.

**Why:** This is the primary differentiator from Indian D2C streetwear brands that lead with discount banners and dense product walls. PREPPY LOSERS sells a world first; commerce follows.

**Applied to:** Homepage layout, collection intros, launch creative, social landing pages.

---

### 2. Fabric Is the Hero

Lead with material truth — GSM weight, weave, hand-feel, construction — before aesthetics or price.

**Why:** Indian premium streetwear buyers are increasingly skeptical of markup without substance. Bluorng and others claim quality; PREPPY LOSERS must prove it transparently and make craft the conversion argument.

**Applied to:** Product descriptions, PDP layout, hangtag content, campaign copy, comparison content.

---

### 3. Scarcity Must Be Real

Limited drops are documented, numbered, and never restocked as "limited edition" theater. When it's gone, it's gone.

**Why:** Fake scarcity is endemic in Indian ecommerce. Real drop integrity builds cult loyalty and protects brand equity — the audience will call out restock bait instantly on Instagram and Reddit.

**Applied to:** Inventory strategy, drop pages, sold-out states, email comms, social announcements.

---

### 4. Restraint Over Noise

Remove before adding. One strong idea per screen. No visual or copy clutter competing for attention.

**Why:** Indian streetwear digital experiences tend toward loud graphics, sale pop-ups, and emoji-heavy copy. Restraint signals premium and editorial confidence — the brand trusts its work to speak.

**Applied to:** UI design, motion design, homepage hierarchy, notification strategy, ad creative.

---

### 5. Mobile Is the Primary Stage

Design, performance, and checkout are optimized for mobile-first discovery — Instagram → site → purchase on phone.

**Why:** India's streetwear audience discovers and buys on mobile. A beautiful desktop site that fails on a ₹20,000 Android phone loses the market. Premium must not mean slow or desktop-only.

**Applied to:** Responsive breakpoints, image sizing, checkout flow, payment integration (UPI-first), page weight budgets.

---

### 6. Underground, Not Imported

The brand references global street culture but speaks from an Indian creative perspective — casting, contexts, and cultural references must feel lived-in, not cosplayed.

**Why:** Indian youth can spot borrowed aesthetics instantly. Authenticity comes from local creative credibility (music scenes, skate communities, art colleges, metro subcultures), not from copying Western lookbooks.

**Applied to:** Photography casting, campaign locations, collaborations, social content, About page narrative.

---

### 7. Every Touchpoint Is Premium

From 404 pages to order confirmation emails to return labels — nothing should feel like an afterthought or a default template.

**Why:** Indian D2C brands often invest in homepage hero but neglect post-purchase and edge states. Premium is a system, not a landing page. One cheap touchpoint breaks the illusion.

**Applied to:** Transactional emails, error states, loading states, packaging design, invoice presentation, customer support tone.

---

### 8. Contradiction Is the Compass

When facing a design or product decision, lean into the paradox — refined yet raw, exclusive yet inviting, minimal yet bold. If the answer feels safe or generic, it's wrong.

**Why:** The brand name itself is a contradiction. That tension is the creative engine. Resolving it into something comfortable kills what makes PREPPY LOSERS memorable.

**Applied to:** Naming, collection themes, visual juxtapositions, copy tone, collaboration selection, art direction.

**Why:** Emotion is the product for fashion brands. Transaction is the outcome. Design and copy must engineer feeling first.

## Website Experience Goals

This section defines the intended emotional and functional journey from landing page to checkout. It bridges brand strategy (what we want users to feel) with UX execution (how the site delivers it).

### Journey Overview

```
LANDING → DISCOVERY → IMMERSION → CONVICTION → COMMITMENT → CONFIRMATION
   ↓           ↓            ↓            ↓             ↓              ↓
Intrigue    Desire      Confidence   Exclusivity    Trust         Belonging
```

---

### Stage 1: Landing (Homepage / Campaign Entry)

**Entry points:** Instagram link, drop alert, direct URL, search, word of mouth

**Emotional goal:** Intrigue — the visitor pauses and thinks, *"This isn't a normal clothing site."*

**Experience characteristics:**
- Full-viewport editorial hero — campaign imagery, not a product carousel
- Minimal navigation visible; atmosphere leads
- Tagline or drop statement appears with restraint — no explainer paragraphs
- Scroll invites exploration; does not overwhelm with categories
- Site loads fast on mobile — premium never feels broken or slow

**Success signal:** User scrolls past the hero. Time on page exceeds 15 seconds. Bounce rate lower than category average.

**Why:** First impressions in India are brutal — users decide in seconds whether a brand is "mid" or worth their attention. The homepage must earn the scroll.

---

### Stage 2: Discovery (Shop / Collections / Drop Page)

**Emotional goal:** Desire — *"I want to be in this world. I want these pieces."*

**Experience characteristics:**
- Collections presented as editorial chapters, not SKU grids
- Large imagery, generous whitespace, minimal UI chrome
- Drop context visible — season name, theme, limited nature implied
- Filtering available but unobtrusive — never the primary visual element
- Hover/tap interactions feel tactile and confident, not playful

**Success signal:** User views 3+ pieces. Enters at least one product detail page.

**Why:** Discovery is where editorial positioning converts curiosity into intent. Indian competitors show products; PREPPY LOSERS shows a culture.

---

### Stage 3: Immersion (Product Detail Page)

**Emotional goal:** Confidence — *"This is me. The quality is real. I know what I'm buying."*

**Experience characteristics:**
- Hero product photography — full-bleed, editorial, zoomable
- Fabric and construction details prominently displayed (GSM, composition, fit type)
- Size guidance clear — oversized fit explained with model measurements
- Price visible but not leading — craft story comes first
- Sticky add-to-cart on mobile — frictionless but not aggressive
- Related pieces feel curated, not algorithmic

**Success signal:** User adds to cart or saves to wishlist. Scroll depth reaches fabric/construction section.

**Why:** Indian premium buyers research heavily before purchasing. The PDP is the trust-building engine — it must answer "why does this cost ₹4,999?" without being asked.

---

### Stage 4: Conviction (Cart / Pre-Checkout)

**Emotional goal:** Exclusivity — *"I'm getting something not everyone will have."*

**Experience characteristics:**
- Cart feels like a curated selection, not a shopping basket
- Product thumbnails maintain editorial quality at small size
- No surprise fees — shipping and taxes transparent before checkout
- Limited stock messaging only when genuine — never manufactured urgency
- Wishlist/save accessible for drop savers who aren't ready yet

**Success signal:** User proceeds to checkout without abandoning due to surprise costs or confusion.

**Why:** Cart abandonment in India is often driven by hidden shipping, COD uncertainty, and distrust. Transparency is premium behavior.

---

### Stage 5: Commitment (Checkout)

**Emotional goal:** Trust — *"This brand respects my time and my money."*

**Experience characteristics:**
- Minimal steps — guest checkout available, no forced account creation
- UPI and popular Indian payment methods prioritized (GPay, PhonePe, Paytm, cards, COD if offered)
- Pincode-based delivery estimate early in flow
- Clean, dark, minimal UI consistent with brand world — not a white generic checkout template
- Order summary always visible; no dark patterns
- Loading states feel intentional, not broken

**Success signal:** Checkout completion rate meets or exceeds premium D2C benchmarks. Low payment failure rate.

**Why:** Checkout is where premium brands often break character. A jarring white payment page destroys the campaign illusion built across the journey.

---

### Stage 6: Confirmation (Order Complete / Post-Purchase)

**Emotional goal:** Belonging — *"I'm in. That was worth it."*

**Experience characteristics:**
- Confirmation page feels like an invitation, not a receipt
- Copy is minimal and assured — "You're in." not "Thank you for your order!!!"
- Order details clear; delivery timeline honest
- Email confirmation matches brand tone and visual system
- Optional: early access signup for next drop — insider, not newsletter spam

**Success signal:** User opens confirmation email. Returns for next drop. Shares unboxing if product delivers.

**Why:** Post-purchase is the beginning of loyalty, not the end of a transaction. In India's word-of-mouth-driven streetwear culture, the confirmation moment sets up the Instagram story.

---

### Cross-Journey Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **No dead ends** | Every page offers a clear next step — deeper into the world or toward purchase |
| **Consistent atmosphere** | Dark, editorial, minimal — from homepage to checkout to email |
| **Performance is premium** | Slow = cheap. Every stage must load fast on 4G mobile |
| **Respect the scroll** | Editorial pacing; reveal content progressively, never dump everything at once |
| **Commerce is invisible until needed** | Cart, size selectors, and checkout appear when intent is clear — not before |

**Why:** A disjointed journey — editorial homepage, generic checkout, corporate email — is the fastest way to lose trust with a premium-aware Indian audience.

Metrics are grouped into three layers — Business, Experience, and Technical — each supporting the others. A beautiful site that doesn't convert fails. A fast site that feels generic fails. A converting site with no retention fails.

### Technical Goals

Technical Goals ensure the editorial, image-rich experience remains fast and accessible on Indian mobile networks. Performance is a brand promise — slow sites signal cheap brands.

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| **Lighthouse Performance** | > 90 (mobile and desktop) | Editorial sites fail when they lag. Premium perception requires sub-3s perceived load |
| **Lighthouse Accessibility** | > 95 | Inclusive design is non-negotiable; also supports SEO and legal compliance |
| **Lighthouse SEO** | > 90 | Organic discovery for brand name, drop pages, and collection pages |
| **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)** | < 2.5s | Hero imagery must load fast — LCP is typically the campaign hero image on 4G |
| **First Input Delay (FID)** | Within Core Web Vitals thresholds (< 100ms) | Interactions (nav, ATC, scroll) must feel instant; note: INP (< 200ms) is the current CWV interaction metric and should also be monitored |
| **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)** | < 0.1 | Editorial layouts with large images must not jump during load — breaks premium feel |

#### Technical Guardrails

- All hero and PDP images served via modern formats (WebP/AVIF) with responsive srcset
- Critical CSS inlined; non-critical assets deferred
- Font loading optimized (subset, preload, `font-display: swap`)
- Third-party scripts (analytics, payments) loaded asynchronously — never block render
- Test on throttled 4G (India profile) before every release — not just office WiFi

**Why:** Indian users on mobile networks will not wait for a beautiful site that takes 8 seconds to load. Technical goals protect the brand experience at the infrastructure level. Document 09 (Technology Decisions) will specify the stack that achieves these targets.

## Brand Voice

### Voice Definition

PREPPY LOSERS speaks like a **culturally sharp insider** who doesn't need to prove anything. The voice is:

- **Direct** — short sentences, no fluff
- **Assured** — statements, not questions (unless rhetorical)
- **Understated** — never oversells; the product and imagery do the heavy lifting
- **Contemporary** — current without slang that ages in six months
- **Editorial** — could live in a fashion magazine sidebar

### Voice Is NOT

- Corporate ("We are pleased to inform you...")
- Hype-beast ("FIRE DROP!!! COP NOW 🔥")
- Condescending ("If you know, you know" used as gatekeeping)
- Overly poetic (purple prose that slows comprehension)
- Generic DTC ("Elevate your wardrobe with our premium essentials")

**Why:** Voice consistency builds recognition. A customer should identify PREPPY LOSERS copy the same way they identify the silhouette.

## Writing Tone

### Tone Matrix by Context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| **Homepage hero** | Cinematic, sparse | "Underground street culture for the bold." |
| **Collection intro** | Editorial, atmospheric | "Drop 01 — built heavy, worn loose." |
| **Product description** | Factual + evocative | "450 GSM fleece. Oversized fit. Drop-shoulder construction. Feels like armor." |
| **CTA buttons** | Confident, minimal | "Shop the drop" / "View piece" / "Enter" |
| **Cart / checkout** | Clear, respectful | "Your selection" / "Complete order" |
| **Error states** | Calm, human | "Something went wrong. Try again." |
| **Email — drop alert** | Insider, urgent-but-cool | "Drop 04 is live. Limited run." |
| **Email — order confirm** | Premium, reassuring | "You're in. Order confirmed." |
| **About page** | Manifesto energy | First-person plural, conviction, no corporate mission fluff |
| **FAQ** | Straightforward | Answers without brand performance |

### Tone Principles

1. **Lowercase can be used intentionally** for headlines and CTAs — but not everywhere. Use as a stylistic signature, not a default.
2. **Avoid exclamation marks** except rare campaign moments. They cheapen premium tone.
3. **Numbers over adjectives** when describing product — "450 GSM" beats "super thick."
4. **Never apologize** for exclusivity — but never mock the customer either.

**Why:** Tone modulates by context while voice stays constant. This prevents the brand from sounding like a different company on checkout vs. homepage.

## Messaging Guidelines

### Core Message Hierarchy

```
Level 1 — Brand Promise
"Underground street culture for the bold."

Level 2 — Supporting Pillars
→ Premium oversized streetwear
→ Heavyweight quality you can feel
→ Limited drops, not endless inventory
→ Self-expression over conformity

Level 3 — Proof Points
→ Fabric weights, construction details, fit guidance
→ Drop dates, edition sizes, collection narratives
→ Editorial photography and campaign world
```

### Message Rules

| Rule | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| Lead with feeling, support with fact | Fashion sells emotion; facts close the rational gap |
| One idea per sentence in UI copy | Scannability on mobile; editorial ≠ verbose |
| Never lead with price | Premium brands lead with value and identity |
| Use "drop" not "sale" | Reinforces event culture |
| Use "piece" or "item" over "product" in customer-facing copy | More editorial, less transactional |
| Avoid "essential" / "basics" / "must-have" | Generic DTC language; undermines exclusivity |

### Campaign Messaging Framework

Every campaign or drop should answer three questions:

1. **What is this?** — Collection name, season, theme
2. **Why does it exist?** — Cultural or creative reason (one line)
3. **Why now?** — Drop timing, limited nature, relevance

**Why:** Messaging hierarchy prevents random copy decisions and keeps all communication aligned to the brand promise.

## Target Audience

### Primary Audience Definition

**Culture-forward outsiders aged 18–32 in India** who spend on fashion as identity expression, follow underground music, art, skate, and hip-hop scenes, and reject mass-market aesthetics — but have or aspire to premium taste and disposable income for clothing.

### Audience Segments

| Segment | Description | Motivation |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **The Creative Outsider** | Designer, musician, artist, content creator | Clothing as personal brand extension |
| **The Tasteful Hype Consumer** | Knows drops, follows Indian and global streetwear, buys selectively | Exclusivity + quality over volume |
| **The Style Escapee** | Escaped fast fashion (Bewakoof, H&M), building intentional wardrobe | Investment pieces that signal upgrade |
| **The Subculture Native** | Skate, hip-hop, desi underground music rooted | Authenticity and cultural respect matter deeply |

### Psychographic Profile

- Values **authenticity** over popularity
- Willing to pay premium for **quality and exclusivity**
- Discovers brands through **Instagram, YouTube, friends, Reddit (r/streetwearindia), and creator culture** — not Google search for "buy hoodie online"
- Judges brands by **website experience** as much as product
- Hates **corporate tone, fake scarcity, logo-heavy flexing, and Flipkart-grade UX**
- Responds to **editorial storytelling and clean design**
- Expects **UPI payment, transparent shipping, and honest sizing** as baseline

### Geographic Focus (Launch)

- **Primary:** Metro India — Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, Kolkata, Chandigarh
- **Secondary:** Tier-2 culture hubs — Jaipur, Indore, Goa, Kochi, Ahmedabad (college + creative communities)
- **Language:** English first — Hinglish acceptable in social/campaign contexts, not in core UI copy
- **Expansion path:** Architecture should support future regional language without redesign

**Why:** Narrow targeting enables bold creative decisions. India's streetwear market is large but taste-aware — a brand for everyone looks like a brand for no one.

## Competitor Positioning

### Market Context: Indian Premium Streetwear

India's streetwear market is projected to exceed $20B by 2033, driven by Gen Z and young millennials who treat fashion as identity. The homegrown landscape splits into three tiers:

1. **Mass-accessible streetwear** — Bewakoof, The Souled Store (volume, discounts, broad appeal)
2. **Growth D2C streetwear** — Bonkers Corner, Snitch, Urban Monkey (scale, marketing-heavy, omnichannel)
3. **Premium / luxury-leaning streetwear** — Bluorng, Almost Gods, PREPPY LOSERS's tier (quality, exclusivity, editorial)

PREPPY LOSERS competes in tier 3 but must differentiate through **editorial digital experience**, **underground positioning**, and **campaign-grade brand world** — not just fabric weight alone.

## Selling Proposition

### Unique Selling Proposition (USP)

> **Heavyweight, campaign-grade streetwear for India's bold outsiders** — limited drops of premium oversized pieces built with documented fabric weight and intentional craftsmanship, presented through an editorial experience that feels like an underground fashion campaign, not an online store.

The USP rests on three non-negotiable pillars:

1. **Heavyweight fabric quality** — Every piece specifies GSM, composition, and construction. Premium is proven, not claimed. 400+ GSM fleece, structured cotton, reinforced seams — quality you can feel before you read the price.

2. **Real limited drops** — Documented edition sizes. No permanent catalog. No fake restock cycles. When a drop closes, it closes. Scarcity is structural, not marketing.

3. **Premium craftsmanship** — Intentional pattern-making for oversized silhouettes. Drop-shoulder construction. Bar-tacked stress points. Premium hardware. Every detail justifies the investment and survives beyond one season.

### Supporting Value Propositions

| Proposition | Customer Benefit | Proof |
|-------------|------------------|-------|
| **Heavyweight fabric quality** | Investment that feels and lasts | GSM weights, fabric composition, care instructions on every PDP |
| **Real limited drops** | Owning something genuinely scarce | Documented edition sizes, no restock policy, sold-out integrity |
| **Premium craftsmanship** | Construction that matches the price | Construction details, seam types, fit engineering, quality photography |
| **Editorial experience** | Shopping feels cultural, not transactional | Campaign-first site design, immersive collection pages |
| **Underground authenticity** | Brand aligns with subcultural identity | Local casting, cultural credibility, paradox positioning |
| **Identity alignment** | Clothes match who they are | Brand voice, self-expression values, outsider narrative |

### Reasons to Believe (RTBs)

1. **Fabric transparency** — specific GSM weights, compositions, and care instructions on every piece (not "premium fabric" vagueness)
2. **Drop integrity** — documented edition sizes per drop; published sold-out states; no fake scarcity restocks
3. **Construction proof** — detailed craftsmanship breakdown: seam type, fit engineering, hardware, wash treatment
4. **Design consistency** — every touchpoint (site, email, packaging, tags) feels like the same brand world
5. **Performance standards** — fast, accessible, mobile-first on Indian networks (premium ≠ slow)
6. **Cultural credibility** — underground roots in creative direction; Indian subcultural casting and contexts

### Elevator Pitch (Internal)

PREPPY LOSERS is premium oversized streetwear for India's bold outsiders. We drop limited pieces in heavyweight fabrics with intentional craftsmanship, presented through an editorial digital experience that feels like an underground fashion campaign — not another Indian D2C store.

**Why:** The USP is the conversion backbone. Every page, feature, and design decision should reinforce heavyweight quality, limited drops, and premium craftsmanship — the three pillars that separate PREPPY LOSERS from Bluorng (luxury lane), Bonkers Corner (scale lane), and Snitch (volume lane).

## Design Constraints

Design Constraints are hard rules that protect visual identity consistency across all 10 foundation documents and future development. They exist to prevent drift toward generic ecommerce patterns — especially common in the Indian D2C landscape.

### Always Do

| Constraint | Rationale |
|------------|-----------|
| **Use dark, editorial backgrounds as default** | Establishes campaign atmosphere; separates from white-background D2C norm |
| **Lead with photography and whitespace** | Product and imagery are heroes; UI defers |
| **Maintain high typographic contrast** | Bold, confident type hierarchy — readable and striking |
| **Keep navigation minimal and unobtrusive** | Commerce supports discovery; it doesn't dominate |
| **Use generous spacing between elements** | Premium brands breathe; density signals cheap |
| **Show fabric and construction details prominently** | Craft is a core USP; make it visible in design, not just copy |
| **Design mobile-first for every component** | India's audience discovers and buys on phone |
| **Maintain brand atmosphere through checkout** | Don't break the editorial world at the point of payment |
| **Use lowercase or sentence case for headlines** | Confident restraint; not shouting |
| **Keep CTAs short and assured** | "Shop the drop" / "View piece" — not "Buy Now!!!" |
| **Ensure fast load on 4G connections** | Performance is a design constraint, not an engineering afterthought |
| **Use real product photography only** | No stock images, no generic mockups, no AI-generated models |
| **Design sold-out and waitlist states with intention** | Scarcity moments are brand moments, not dead ends |
| **Keep payment UI clean with UPI prominence** | Indian checkout reality; GPay/PhonePe/PhonePe are expected |

---

### Never Do

| Constraint | Rationale |
|------------|-----------|
| **Never use bright, saturated primary colors** | Conflicts with dark editorial premium world |
| **Never use large gradients or gradient backgrounds** | Reads as startup/SaaS, not fashion editorial |
| **Never use rounded, playful UI (pill buttons, bubbly cards)** | Conflicts with bold-minimal luxury streetwear tone |
| **Never use generic ecommerce layouts (sidebar filters + dense product grid as homepage)** | Primary anti-pattern; this is what we're building against |
| **Never show sale banners, discount pop-ups, or coupon codes on arrival** | Destroys premium positioning instantly |
| **Never use countdown timers for fake urgency** | Indian audience is skeptical; fake urgency erodes trust |
| **Never use exclamation marks in UI copy** | Cheapens premium voice |
| **Never use emoji in interface copy** | Bonkers Corner energy; not PREPPY LOSERS |
| **Never use carousel-heavy hero with multiple CTAs** | Editorial brands have one strong moment, not a slideshow of offers |
| **Never use white checkout pages that break brand atmosphere** | Jarring transition destroys the campaign illusion |
| **Never use stock photography or generic fashion models** | Breaks authenticity; underground brands cast real people |
| **Never over-animate (bounce, wiggle, parallax overload)** | Motion must be confident and restrained, not playful |
| **Never use logo-as-hero (giant logo on every page)** | Product and campaign lead; logo is a signature, not the headline |
| **Never use "Best Seller" / "Trending" / "Must Have" badges** | Generic D2C language; undermines exclusivity |
| **Never use light mode as default** | Light backgrounds signal accessible/mass-market in this brand context |
| **Never use more than two typefaces in the system** | Typographic restraint is premium |
| **Never lead any page with price** | Value and identity come before cost |
| **Never use corporate trust badges excessively (Norton, Visa logos dominating footer)** | Minimal footer; trust is earned through experience, not badge walls |

---

### Constraint Hierarchy

When constraints conflict during design decisions, resolve in this order:

1. **Brand Principles** (Document 01) — highest authority
2. **Design Constraints — Never Do** — hard guardrails
3. **Design Constraints — Always Do** — strong defaults
4. **Visual Direction** (Document 02) — specific token values
5. **Component-level flexibility** — lowest authority

**Why:** Without explicit constraints, every future developer and designer will drift toward familiar patterns — white backgrounds, rounded buttons, sale banners — because those are the defaults of ecommerce tooling. These lists are the immune system against generic.
